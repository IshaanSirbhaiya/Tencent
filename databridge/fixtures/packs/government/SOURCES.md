# SOURCES -- what each source in the government estate can honestly promise

> **SYNTHETIC FIXTURE.** The Aldermoor Territory, its agencies and every record
> in this estate are invented. Generated by `generate.py`.

This is the content of `describe()` for each source, written down before any
adapter exists, because the whole point of `describe()` is that the
orchestrator can plan **honestly** — and it can only do that if a source is
made to admit what it cannot do.

The machine-readable form of everything below is **[`sources.yaml`](sources.yaml)**,
which is the artefact an adapter's `describe()` returns. This file is its prose
companion; where they appear to disagree, `sources.yaml` is the contract.

Each entry declares what `ADAPTER_AGENT_SPEC.md` §3 requires:

| Field | What it means | Why lying about it is expensive |
|---|---|---|
| **pinning tier** | T0–T3 (spine D6) | a `snapshot(at=<past>)` against a source that cannot serve one must **raise**, not improvise |
| **delete detectable** | can the adapter tell a deleted row from an absent one | if not, absence is not evidence, and an adapter that infers deletion is fabricating |
| **watermark** | is there a cursor that never goes backwards *and never skips* | an unreliable watermark silently skips rows on every incremental read |
| **effective retention** | how far back the source *actually* serves — not the documented figure | the documented number is marketing; the effective one is what the pointer is worth |
| **origin / trust / egress** | three *independent* axes — see below | conflating them is how third-party data ends up trusted, or public data ends up leaking a case |

**Government is the T2/T3-heavy estate.** Finance is the T0/T1 counterpart — a
system of record with real snapshots and a real LSN. Here, half the sources have
no history at all, and the ones that do only have it going forward, for two
tables each. That asymmetry is the point of having both: an architecture that
only works against Iceberg tables has not been tested.

## The three classification axes

`third_party_ingress` used to appear in the egress column of this table. It does
not belong there: it says where data *came from* and how much it may be
believed, not where it may *go*. The two are independent — third-party data is
frequently the most restricted (it contains other people's personal data), and
internally produced data is frequently the least (a statutory public register).
They are now three separate declarations.

**`origin_class`** — `agency_internal` · `third_party_ingress` · `public_source`

**`trust_class`** (spine D3) — `trusted` · `untrusted`. An untrusted value may
be stored, cited and displayed; it may **never influence control flow**. Trust
flows up the lattice and never down: anything derived from an untrusted parent
is untrusted. Stamped by the adapter Core from this contract, never inferred
from content — that would let the thing being contained decide whether it is
contained.

**`egress_class`** — `PUBLIC` · `INTERNAL` · `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL`. Where the
bytes may go. Enforced at pointer resolution against the reading role's
`egress_ceiling` in `roles.yaml`.

**How they combine.** A derived output takes the **maximum** egress class and
the **least trusted** trust class over all its inputs. Joining open parcel data
(`PUBLIC`) to an applicant row (`RESTRICTED_INTERNAL`) yields
`RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` — the join *is* the disclosure, and the open half does not
launder the restricted half. "This parcel is zoned R2" is public; "this parcel
is the site of APP-2023-000252" is not.

## Trust is a field-level property

A single source-level trust bit launders exactly the fields that most need
containing. Both PostgreSQL databases default to `trusted` and both carry
columns that are not:

| Source | Untrusted fields | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `permits_registry` | `applicants.notes`, `applications.site_description`, `applications.agent_of_record`, `decisions.rationale`, `inspections.notes`, `appeals.grounds`, `case_events.detail` | free text keyed from what a member of the public said or wrote |
| `benefits_case_management` | `claim_notes.body`, `claim_events.detail`, `reviews.notes`, `payments.reference`, `eligibility_determinations.evidence_ref` | ditto — and **two of the estate's ten planted injections were pasted into `claim_notes.body`** by a caseworker copying an email |
| `inspection_photos` | `ocr.blocks[].text` | a placard photographed on site. The sidecar's `inspection_id`, `photo_batch_ref`, `captured_by` and `captured_at` come from the agency's own device fleet and *are* trusted |

**Passing through a system of record does not promote trust.** That laundering
path — untrusted email → trusted database column → trusted graph edge — is the
one this estate exists to catch, and it is why `AGENT_FOR` (derived from
`applications.agent_of_record`) is a **proposal edge** rather than an assertion.

## What `VERIFIED` actually asserts

A `VERIFIED` verdict means *the bytes currently at this locator equal the
captured bytes*. It is a present-tense claim. It does **not** establish that the
bytes were there when the citation was made — only a T0 source or a Capture
Store entry can establish that. Every source declares its own reading in
`sources.yaml: verified_means`, because for an unversioned source the two claims
are very different and the difference is the whole value of a citation.

This is a real ambiguity in the D6 lattice as written, not a fixture quirk; it
is recorded in `wiki/40-reviews/R-002-government-estate-fixes.md`.

**Subject kinds present:** `Person` (in the relationship roles *applicant* and
*appellant*), `Organisation` (as *agent*). See `ONTOLOGY_NOTES.md`.

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T2 source-wide.** One stream, `case_events`, is **T1** under an explicit cursor protocol. The source tier is the minimum over its streams. |
| Why not T1 source-wide | Only `case_events` has any cursor at all. Updates and deletes in `applicants`, `applications`, `decisions`, `permit_conditions`, `inspections` and `appeals` are entirely unobservable, so a source-wide T1 claim would promise changes the adapter cannot see. **This deployment runs no logical replication slot**, so there is no retained LSN to read either — and an LSN PostgreSQL can *report* is not a readable, retained change feed. |

| `DRIFT_BENIGN` | **never emitted.** It requires an entity-level change signal covering the whole row (`xmin`, a row version) that this adapter does not read. Per `ADAPTER_AGENT_SPEC` §5, an adapter that cannot compute it does not emit it. |

### The `case_events` cursor protocol — why it takes three parts

`recorded_at` is not a safe cursor merely because it normally increases. A clock
is not a cursor, and it fails in two distinct ways:

- **equal timestamps** hide rows behind one another, so a watermark that moves
  past a timestamp skips everything else that shares it;
- **commit-order races**: a transaction takes its timestamp early and commits
  late. A watermark that has already advanced past that timestamp will never
  read it. The row is lost silently, forever, with no error and no gap in any
  sequence.

So the stream is T1 **only** with all three of:

1. a **compound key** `(recorded_at, event_id)`, strict lexicographic — breaks
   ties deterministically;
2. an **overlap window** of 30 minutes, which must exceed the longest permitted
   transaction duration — catches the commit-order race, with `event_id` as the
   dedupe key;
3. a **stabilisation lag** of 5 minutes — never read rows newer than
   `now() - 5min`; they may still be in flight.

Omit any of the three and the cursor is lossy and the stream is **T2**, not T1.
`event_id` alone does not save it: a sequence value is obtained *before* commit,
so it is a tiebreaker, not a commit order.

**And `occurred_at` is never the cursor.** It is valid time, and it routinely
trails `recorded_at` by up to eleven days because counter staff key events in
batches. An adapter that watermarks on `occurred_at` will silently lose rows.

### What would raise the tier

**To T1 source-wide:** a retained logical replication slot with a publication
covering every table; declared slot retention and a *tested, non-silent*
handling of slot expiry; tombstones surfaced from the slot, so `deletes_detectable`
can move off `none`; and a declared bootstrap-snapshot → slot handoff protocol
with no gap and no duplicate at the seam.

**To T0** — see below. PITR alone is not enough.

**Traps deliberately built in**

- `applications.parcel_id` has no foreign key to the GIS. It is a different
  system on a different release train, and a small tail of `PCL-XX-*` values
  resolve to nothing. An adapter that inner-joins these away is deleting evidence.
- `applicants.national_id_ref` is nullable and frequently wrong. It is not a
  join key, however much it looks like one.
- Roughly 6% of people have two applicant rows. The duplicates are within one
  source and one table.

## 2. `benefits_case_management` — CLAIMLINE

**Kind:** PostgreSQL 14+. `schema.sql` + `seed/load_all.sql`.
**Subject kinds present:** `Person` (relationship role *claimant*).

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T2 source-wide.** Two streams are **T1** under the same protocol as permits: `claim_events` and `payments`. |
| Why not T1 source-wide | `claimants`, `claims`, `eligibility_determinations`, `reviews` and `claim_notes` have no cursor, no tombstone and no change feed. |
| Delete detectable | **No.** Determinations supersede and payments reverse; everything else can vanish without trace. |
| Watermark | `claim_events` on `(recorded_at, event_id)` and `payments` on `(posted_at, payment_id)` — both only under the compound-key + overlap + stabilisation protocol. `payment_id` is text and **not monotonic**; it is a deterministic tiebreaker only, and ordering is by `posted_at` first. |
| Effective retention | **~7 years of events.** An unlogged retention job truncates `claim_events`, so the oldest claims have determinations and payments with **no surviving events**. Absence of an event is not evidence the event did not happen — the single most important line in this file, because it is the mistake a graph builder makes by default. It also means a full resync from cursor zero returns *less* than one run last year: the cursor is forward-only **and** its tail is being eaten. |
| Origin / trust / egress | `agency_internal` / `trusted` (field overrides above) / `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` |
| Capabilities | `describe`, `snapshot` (present only), `plan`, `resolve`, `verify` |

**The entity-resolution test lives here.** CLAIMLINE never adopted the Aldermoor
Resident Number; it keys on its own BRN. Roughly 55% of the people in it are the
same human beings as rows in `permits.applicants`, and *nothing* joins the two
reliably: `resident_number` is present on about a third of rows, hand-keyed, and
often transposed or truncated; `claims.linked_application_id` is a hint on about
12% of claims, mistyped on a quarter of those. The answer key is
`GROUND_TRUTH.md`.

## What T0 would actually take

Both databases could in principle become T0. Enabling backups would not do it.
The full bar is:

- an **operational restore-or-query service** that can serve a requested past
  instant on demand — not a runbook, a service;
- a **declared recoverable window** *and* a **declared granularity**
  (per-transaction, or per-N-minutes);
- **transaction-consistent handling across both databases**, because the graph
  joins them. Two independently restored databases give a torn read that looks
  like a determination without a payment;
- retention measured in **actually-restorable, restore-tested backups**, not in
  configured WAL retention;
- a **tested failure mode**: what `snapshot(at=...)` returns when the requested
  instant falls outside the window.

Until every one of those is true of the deployment, `snapshot(at=<past>)` raises
`CannotPinPast`. Claiming T0 on the strength of a backup schedule is exactly the
tier inflation that makes a citation worthless three months after it is made.

## 3. `case_files` — scanned document stand-ins

**Kind:** `.md` / `.txt` OCR text under `<year>/`, plus `INDEX.csv`.

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T3** — unversioned filesystem drop |
| Delete detectable | **No.** A file that disappears is indistinguishable from one that was never indexed. |
| Watermark | **None usable.** `mtime` moves when a file is re-scanned, re-saved or copied by a backup tool, and does *not* move when content is replaced in place by some scanners. It is wrong in both directions. |
| Effective retention | Indefinite, but **unversioned**: the previous OCR pass is gone the moment a page is re-scanned. |
| Origin / trust / egress | `third_party_ingress` / `untrusted` (all fields) / `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` |
| Capabilities | `describe`, `plan`, `resolve`, `verify` — against the Capture Store, and a *live-bytes* check besides |
| Capture rule | Anything ledger-bound is captured. A pointer into a case file resolves against the **Capture Store** (content-addressed, WORM), never the live path. |

**There is something to verify against, and it is worth having.** An earlier
version of this file said case files had "nothing to verify against". That
discarded a real distinction. A live re-read and digest comparison establishes
that *the bytes currently at this path equal the captured bytes* — which is
exactly how a silent re-scan is **detected**. It does not establish that the
bytes were there at citation time; that is what `VERIFIED_AS_OF_CAPTURE` against
the Capture Store is for, and it remains the strongest verdict this tier reaches.

**`case_ref` is a claim, not a routing decision.** The `case_ref` column in
`INDEX.csv` is recovered from OCR of a form a member of the public filled in.
The adapter exposes it as **`claimed_case_ref`**, untrusted. It may be stored,
cited and displayed; it may not be a row predicate, may not create a traversable
`EVIDENCES` edge, and may not decide who can see the document. A trusted
`case_ref` exists only after an agency intake mapping or a named human
attestation.

**The OCR damage is deliberate** — character confusions, mid-word hyphenation
from re-flowed wrapping, page furniture, misread identifiers. Some values are
genuinely unrecoverable, and the correct output for those is `unresolved`, not a
repaired guess.

## 4. `legacy_records` — LEGACY-MASTER

**Kind:** fixed-width flat file, 220 bytes/record, with a COBOL copybook
(`COPYBOOK.cpy`, `LAYOUT.md`).

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T3** — the archetype |
| Delete detectable | **No.** The file is re-cut in full nightly. Between two cuts there is no diff, no tombstone and no key stability guarantee. |
| Watermark | **None.** `LM-LAST-UPD-CYMD` is a date, not a cursor: day granularity, and some records carry `00000000`. |
| Effective retention | **One generation.** Only the most recent cut exists. Yesterday's is gone. |
| Origin / trust / egress | `agency_internal` / `trusted` as to provenance, worthless as to currency / `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` |
| Capabilities | `describe`, `plan`, `resolve`, `verify` against the Capture Store. No `snapshot`. |
| Best verdict achievable | `VERIFIED_AS_OF_CAPTURE`, or `UNVERIFIABLE` — and `UNVERIFIABLE` is **not a failure**, it is the tier answering honestly |

A live re-read is *possible* here but nearly always meaningless: record offsets
are not stable across nightly cuts, so a byte-range pointer into last night's
file addresses different data today. Cite the capture, never the live file.

**Why this source is worth having.** Every hard problem in government data lives
in it at once: surnames hard-truncated at 30 characters, uppercase-only so
casing carries no signal, dates that are impossible (`19980230`), blank primary
keys, retired reference schemes that do not match the modern ones, and ~8%
orphan records for people no modern system holds. A resolver that forces every
legacy row onto a known person is wrong, and this source is how you find that out.

## 5. `correspondence` — mailbox extract

**Kind:** RFC 5322 `.eml` under `<year>/`, plus `INDEX.csv` and `INJECTIONS.md`.

**The tier is declared twice, on purpose.**

| | As shipped | The live provider contract |
|---|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T3** | **T1** |
| What it is | an unversioned directory drop with a static index | a forward-only history cursor that expires |
| Delete detectable | no | no — a deleted message leaves no tombstone; the cursor simply never mentions it again |
| Retention | bodies indefinite | **~30 days of cursor** (the *documented* figure for comparable providers is "typically at least one week"; the effective figure is the one to plan against) |
| Status | this is what an adapter is actually tested against | **NOT EXERCISED BY THIS FIXTURE** |

An earlier version of this file declared the source T1 outright. That was a
claim made in prose and tested by nothing: a directory of `.eml` files cannot
demonstrate cursor advancement, cursor expiry, a 404, a resync race, or a
message deleted between pages. Before any adapter may claim it implements the T1
contract, the fixture needs a deterministic mock history endpoint serving a
fixed response sequence covering: three pages of cursor advancement; expiry
mid-read (404 → `EXPIRED`, distinguishable from `VANISHED`); duplicate delivery
of one `message_id` across consecutive pages; a late arrival; deletion
ambiguity; and a full-resync handoff with no gap and no duplicate at the seam.
That list is in `sources.yaml: correspondence.live_contract.required_to_exercise`.

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Origin / trust / egress | `third_party_ingress` / `untrusted` (all fields) / `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` |
| Capabilities | `describe`, `plan`, `resolve`. `verify` only against the Capture Store. |
| Capture rule | `email_part` pointers resolve against the **Capture Store**. Always. |

### `X-Case-Reference` may not decide anything

This is the sharpest correction in this revision, because the estate was
previously modelling a flow that spine D3 forbids.

`INDEX.csv` carries a `case_ref` column derived from the inbound
`X-Case-Reference` header. That header arrives on mail from applicants,
claimants and third parties: it is **attacker-controlled**. Using it to associate
a message with a case means a forged header can route hostile content into
another person's case *and* decide who is entitled to read it — untrusted input
deciding control flow and access.

The adapter therefore emits **`claimed_case_ref`**, untrusted, with a pointer to
the header span, plus a `CLAIMS_CASE_REF` **proposal** edge that no access
decision and no determination-producing traversal may follow. A *trusted*
`case_ref` is established only by:

- **agency outbound thread** — the message replies (by `In-Reply-To` /
  `References`) to a message the agency itself sent from the case system, so the
  association comes from the agency's own outbound spool record rather than from
  the inbound header. This is the only automatic path; or
- **human attestation** — a named officer binds the message to a case, with
  principal and timestamp recorded.

Until one of those holds, the message is outside every caseload scope. The
matching demo assertion is `forged_case_reference_reaches_no_caseload` in
`roles.yaml`.

**Hostile content is present on purpose.** Planted prompt-injection attempts sit
in message bodies, an HTML alternative part and a JSON attachment. `INDEX.csv`
does not flag which; `INJECTIONS.md` is the answer key.

## 7b. `public_registry_captures` — the shipped snapshots

**These are a separate resource with a separate contract and a separate pointer
namespace** (`capture://public_registry/`), because the fixture ships dated
directories containing two historical snapshots while the live API is
current-only. Left as one source, an adapter built against the fixture could
serve historical captured states the real adapter cannot, and a test could
validate a T0-like fixture adapter while believing it exercised T2 behaviour.

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T0 — within the captured set only.** Addressable at `2026-05-01` and `2026-08-01`; `UNVERIFIABLE` at every other instant. A Capture Store is not a T0 *source*. |
| Granularity | per shipped capture; three months apart. There is nothing between them. |
| Delete detectable | **Hard, within the set.** Four registrations present in May are absent in August and the store can prove it — which says nothing about whether the registrar *deleted* them or merely stopped returning them. |
| `DRIFT_BENIGN` | **emitted here**, and only here: the `meta.etag` block is an entity-level change signal covering the whole record, so benign drift is genuinely detectable rather than invented. |

Two snapshots ship deliberately so `verify()` has something real to return a
verdict about: status changes (`DRIFT_MATERIAL`), a non-projected field moving
(`DRIFT_BENIGN`), four disappearances (`VANISHED`) and six new rows (not drift
at all). The expected verdict for each is in `public_registry/DRIFT.md`.

## 8. `inspection_photos` — image metadata sidecars

**Kind:** JSON sidecars under `sidecars/`, plus `INDEX.csv`. **No images ship**
— every sidecar carries `"image_present": false`.

| Property | Declaration |
|---|---|
| Pinning tier | **T3** |
| Delete detectable | **No.** |
| Watermark | **None.** |
| Effective retention | Indefinite but unversioned; a re-OCR overwrites. |
| Origin / trust / egress | `third_party_ingress` / `untrusted` **for OCR text only** / `RESTRICTED_INTERNAL` |
| Capabilities | `describe`, `plan`, `resolve`, `verify` against the Capture Store |
| Capture rule | `ocr_bbox` pointers resolve against the **Capture Store**, never the live filesystem |

**Which fields may bind a photo to a case.** `inspection_id`, `photo_batch_ref`,
`captured_by` and `captured_at` come from the agency's own device fleet and
batch assignment, and are trusted. The binding path is `photo_batch_ref` →
`permits.inspections.photo_batch_ref` → `inspections.application_id`. The
sidecar's own `application_id` is a convenience copy to be reconciled against
that path, never trusted alone. **OCR text binds nothing.**

### How checkable an `ocr_bbox` pointer really is

An earlier version of this file called the sidecar "exactly what an `ocr_bbox`
pointer needs to be checkable rather than merely plausible". That was misleading:
no images ship, so the sidecar corroborates only itself.

**Checkable:** the sidecar bytes re-read from the Capture Store and match their
digest; the bbox lies within the declared `image.width × image.height`; the
coordinate origin and format are declared (`top_left`, `xywh_pixels`) so the
geometry is unambiguous; block ordering and `line` indices are internally
consistent.

**Not checkable:** that the text was ever present in an image; that it was at
that location in that image; that an image ever existed.

So an `ocr_bbox` pointer here reaches `VERIFIED_AS_OF_CAPTURE` **as to the
sidecar** and `UNVERIFIABLE` **as to the image**, and both must be reported —
reporting only the first would let a reader believe the photograph had been
checked. To make it fully checkable: ship image bytes into the Capture Store,
record an image digest in the sidecar, and make the image retrievable by that
digest so the bbox can be cropped and re-read.

## Cross-cutting notes

**Nothing here is T0 except a Capture Store.** That is the honest reading of the
spine's caution: a source is T0 only if the *deployment* can serve a past
instant. Two PostgreSQL databases could get there; the bar is written out above,
and it is considerably higher than "turn on backups".

**No source in this estate can detect deletes.** Not one — including the two
systems of record, where `soft_only` holds for exactly two tables. Any
incremental strategy that treats absence as deletion will corrupt the graph. The
correct behaviour is to carry the last observation with its capture timestamp
and let `verify()` return `VANISHED` when the pointer is next checked: a verdict
a human can act on, rather than a silent removal nobody sees.

**Five of nine resources are untrusted at the source contract level**
(`correspondence`, `case_files`, `inspection_photos`, `public_registry_api`,
`public_registry_captures`) — and the two trusted databases each carry untrusted
columns. Trust is stamped by the adapter Core from this contract; it is never
inferred from content, because that would let the thing being contained decide
whether it is contained.

**The identity rule is the same across every source.** These systems hold
candidates, not answers. `resolution` presents them; a named human attests;
`requires_human_selection` stays `Literal[True]`. No source in this estate — not
even the two systems of record — is authorised to confirm that two records are
the same person, or the same organisation. `roles.yaml` declares the protected
subject kinds as `Person` and `Organisation`, matching `ONTOLOGY_NOTES.md`.

**What this fixture does not test.** `sources.yaml: known_fixture_gaps` lists ten
adapter failure modes with no coverage here — bootstrap/CDC handoff races, equal
timestamps and out-of-order commits, replication-slot expiry, partial and
duplicated pages, hostile MIME, DST ambiguity, schema drift, multi-role policy
evaluation, purged child history, and permission changes mid-read. No adapter
may claim coverage of any of them on the strength of this estate.
