# Finals video: narration script v5 (FINAL read-aloud version)

v5 = v4 plus the accepted lines from the "make it win" review (review_codex_video_v4.md). The company is
**Company A**, a fictional Singapore fintech; it is named once (Atlas, first line). Every person, company and
case is fictional.

**How to record:**
- A quiet room; a phone or headset mic about 15 cm away.
- One file per block, named as shown (e.g. `B_C4.m4a`), with 2 seconds of silence at the start.
- Read calmly. "·" = a short pause. If you stumble, pause and re-read the whole sentence.

**Running order:** intro (Aaruni, recorded) → AI film (team) → slides (Aaruni, recorded) → **C4 Atlas** → hinge
card → **C5 The Call** → **C6 the company brain** → **C7 close**.

### A_C5: Workflow 2, "The Call" (~70–75 s, the team's speech with the review's edits)
*(If the team's Call recording already has Aaruni's voice, use theirs and skip this block.)*

> **[1]** So meet Amy. · Amy is an account manager, · and she looks after about forty business customers.
>
> **[2]** One of them, Velquarry Trading, · has just called her. · Their account has been frozen, · the
> director is angry, · and he wants to know why. · And Amy doesn't know.
>
> **[3]** Normally that means putting the customer on hold, · and digging through Slack, Jira, Confluence,
> shared drives, emails, SharePoint and databases. · Then messaging compliance, · and waiting for hours.
> And she has to be careful. · If there is a money-laundering investigation behind this, · and Amy even
> hints at it, · that is tipping-off. · That's a crime.
>
> **[4]** So instead, Amy asks Walnut once. · I'm signed in as Amy, · so Walnut only searches what Amy is
> allowed to open.
>
> **[5]** I ask: · "Why has Velquarry Trading's account been restricted, · and what should I tell the
> customer?"
>
> **[6]** And here's the answer: · the approved customer script, · with its sources linked. · Walnut checks
> each source against the source platform at answer time. · No case details. · No hint that a case exists.
>
> **[7]** Walnut also suggests the next actions. · So I draft a reply. · It uses only the approved wording, ·
> and nothing is sent until she chooses to send it herself.
>
> **[8]** Now meet Rachel. · Rachel runs financial crime. · I'm signed in as Rachel now. · Same question, word
> for word. · Watch what her permissions change.
>
> **[9]** And this time it's a completely different answer. · Walnut has pulled the whole case together, ·
> from the case file in Confluence, · the transaction analysis in the drive, · and the investigation
> thread in Slack.
>
> **[10]** And I can turn it into a page. · Thirty-seven transfers in nine days, · why the account is
> restricted, · and what the customer may be told. · I open the citation beside the number, · and show the
> source behind it.
>
> **[11]** Same question. · Different permissions. · Amy gets the script. · Rachel gets the case. · That's
> Walnut.
